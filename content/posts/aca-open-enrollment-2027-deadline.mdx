**Enrol by 15 December 2026.** Every source agrees that is the last day to get
coverage starting 1 January 2027, and no source promises anything after it.

What the sources do not agree on is whether 15 December is also the last day
full stop. The regulation says the federal enrolment period now ends there.
HealthCare.gov's own deadline page still shows a window running to 15 January.
Both were live on 21 November 2026, and most pages ranking for this question
simply pick one and state it as fact.

The same agency publishes the second and third of those.

## Why they disagree

The 2025 Marketplace Integrity and Affordability final rule changed the shape
of the enrolment period. The CMS fact sheet states the general constraint —
"Each OEP must start no later than November 1 and end no later than December
31, and the OEP may not exceed 9 calendar weeks" — and then gives the federal
platform a specific window of 1 November to 15 December, "beginning with the
OEP for plan year 2027."

Plan year 2027 is this one. On the regulation's face, the 16 December to 15
January window and its 1 February coverage start belong to the old timeline.

The most likely explanation for the contradiction is the dullest one: a
deadlines page that was not updated when the rule took effect. We are not in a
position to confirm that, and we are not going to assert it. What we can say is
which page carries the force of a rule and which carries the force of a
web page.

## What to do about it

Treat 15 December as the deadline, for three reasons that do not depend on
resolving the conflict.

All three sources agree it is the cut-off for 1 January coverage. If you need
cover from new year's day, the disputed window is irrelevant to you.

The disputed window is the *optimistic* reading. Acting on 15 December costs
you nothing if 15 January turns out to still exist. Acting on 15 January costs
you the year if it does not.

And a state-based exchange may not follow the federal platform at all. The fact
sheet says the rule "allows all Exchanges flexibility to set their own OEPs
within set parameters for timing and duration," and names no states. If you
enrol through a state marketplace rather than HealthCare.gov, its published
dates govern, and you should read them rather than this page.

## The number that decides your year is not a date

Enrolling is the easy part. The consequential part of the form is the income
you project for 2027, because that projection is what the credit is advanced
against — and what it is reconciled against a year later.

<KeyFigure id="aca.cliff.ceilingMultiple" variant="block" />

Above that line the premium tax credit does not taper. It stops. For 2026
coverage the poverty figure is <KeyFigure id="aca.fpl.firstPerson" /> for one
person plus <KeyFigure id="aca.fpl.additionalPerson" /> for each additional
person, so the ceiling is four times the first figure for a single-person
household, and rises with household size.

What makes a projection error expensive now is a change that landed before this
enrolment season. Advance credit repayment used to be capped for households
under 400% of the poverty line. Those caps were repealed.

<KeyFigure id="aca.clawback.capped" variant="block" />

So a household that enrols in December projecting income under the line, and
finishes 2027 over it — a bonus, a second job, a capital gain, a spouse's raise
— repays the entire year's advance credit. Not a capped portion of it. All of
it. The enrolment deadline is a date you can diarise; this is the one that
takes a year to arrive and arrives in full.
